Privacy Policy

“Charge” app (beta version) · Last updated: October 29, 2026 · Versione italiana

This page explains, in plain language, which data the “Charge” app uses, why, and how we handle it. In Italian the app is called “Carica”: it is the same app, and this policy applies to it in every language. The privacy of your fitness and health information is a priority for us.

1. Who processes your data (Data Controller)

The data controller is Jean-Luc Zaganelli, the independent developer of the “Charge” app. For any question about your data you can write to carica.beta@gmail.com, a mailbox dedicated to the beta. If this mailbox is replaced, the new contact for data requests will be shown here.

2. In short: where your data lives

“Charge” processes your Health data directly on your iPhone and Apple Watch and does not send it to the developer. There are, however, a few specific exceptions, described in detail in sections 3, 5 and 6: the automatic download of terrain maps tells the public service that provides them which geographic tile is being requested; and you may choose, voluntarily, to send a file to the developer during testing, or to write to the developer through the app’s support feature, either with a plain message without health data or, only with a separate explicit consent, by attaching an encrypted diagnostic report containing your health and activity data from the last 28 days (section 6).

3. Which data the app uses

To calculate your “Charge” (a fitness estimate of your energy and recovery level), the app reads, through Apple’s Health app (HealthKit), data such as:

  • heart rate and resting heart rate;
  • heart rate variability;
  • sleep data;
  • workouts, steps and energy burned;
  • location and elevation during workouts (GPS route), to calculate distance and elevation gain.

Under the law these are special categories of data concerning health, and we handle them with the utmost care.

Location when you open the app. If you grant location permission (only “while using the app”, never in the background), Charge reads a single location when you open the app or bring it back to the foreground, at most once every two hours, to find the elevation of the place where you are from the terrain maps. The coordinates are not stored or written to any log: they stay in memory only for the time of the reading. A local history of the last 28 days remains on the device, with the date, the terrain elevation and the reliability of the reading; it is protected like the other local data, with a request to exclude it from device backups (see section 7), and it is deleted when you delete all data. You can deny or revoke the permission at any time: the app keeps working.

4. Why the app uses them, and on what legal basis

The app uses this data only to show you your fitness statistics, your “Charge” and the progress of your workouts. The legal bases are your consent (Article 6(1)(a) GDPR) and, for data concerning health, your explicit consent (Article 9(2)(a) GDPR). Consent is requested at first launch and can be withdrawn in the app’s Settings, as explained in section 8. From build 577 the initial screen asks for two separate confirmations, both switched off at the start: the first to declare that you are an adult, that you have read this policy and that you accept the beta terms of use; the second for your explicit consent to the processing of Health data, to the use of location (only with the system permission) and to the automatic download of terrain maps described in sections 3 and 5. This consent does not authorize sending health data to the developer. In earlier versions a single confirmation covered both the terms and the consent. Health permissions let you choose which categories to make available to the app. Optional sharing of data with support is described in section 6; it does not happen automatically and, from build 577, requires a separate explicit consent given at the time of contact.

The data is not used for advertising, is not sold and is not transferred to third parties for commercial purposes.

5. External services used

  • Apple (HealthKit, TestFlight): the app relies on Apple services; Apple’s terms and privacy policy apply.
  • Terrain maps: to correct the elevation of workouts with a GPS route stored in Health — recorded with Charge or with other apps, for example Apple’s Workout app —, of routes you import yourself, and to find the elevation of the place where you are, the app automatically downloads (also over cellular data) the terrain map tiles it needs from a public open-data service (“Terrain Tiles” on AWS Open Data). That service can see the geographic tile requested and the address of your internet connection, but it does not receive your name, your Health data, your elevation history or any stored coordinates. Downloaded tiles stay on your device until you delete them from the “Elevation Maps” page or delete all of the app’s data.

TestFlight. The developer selects testers, provides Apple with the addresses needed for invitations and manages groups; Apple provides the distribution platform. Apple collects and makes available to the developer information about invitations, installations, app version, device model and operating system, usage sessions and crashes, even without a voluntary report. With an email invitation, this information may be associated with your email address and any name entered in the invitation. If you submit a comment or screenshot through TestFlight, Apple and the developer also receive that content and the technical information described in Apple’s TestFlight privacy notice. Charge does not use its own server for this collection.

The developer uses technical TestFlight information only to check operation, identify problems and improve the beta. For ordinary personal data, the legal basis is the legitimate interest in making the app reliable (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR), limiting analysis to what is necessary for testing. You can object by writing to the address in section 1; you can also stop testing in TestFlight. This basis does not authorize processing health data contained in a screenshot or message. When suggesting features or reporting issues, avoid including health data, routes or other people’s data; any health diagnostic report follows section 6.

Email and services outside the European Economic Area. Applications and messages pass through the sender’s email provider and the developer’s mailbox, currently Gmail. Apple and Google may process data outside the European Economic Area under their respective terms and privacy notices. Google describes its transfer mechanisms, including adequacy decisions and the EU-US framework for covered entities and transfers, in its Legal frameworks for data transfers; Apple describes them in its privacy policy. You can ask the controller about safeguards applicable to the processing they manage. Encryption of the health attachment does not hide the email address or message text from email services.

6. Voluntary sharing during testing (beta)

Applications, invitations and suggestions. If you apply by email, the developer uses your address, any name you choose to provide, your request and necessary correspondence to assess your application, invite you and manage your participation in the free beta. For this ordinary personal data, the legal basis is taking steps at your request before participation and performing the testing relationship once it starts (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR). The address needed for the invitation is provided to Apple through TestFlight. Providing it is optional, but without it a personal email invitation cannot be sent. No identity documents, health data or routes are required to apply. Applications are assessed personally by the developer, without automated decisions about admission.

You can write to carica.beta@gmail.com to suggest improvements and new features or request support. Ordinary support messages are processed to handle your request within the test (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR); improvement proposals are assessed in the legitimate interest of improving the app (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR), with a right to object. Do not use these channels to send unsolicited health information or other people’s data. The carica.beta@gmail.com mailbox is dedicated to the beta: it is used for applications, invitations, support, suggestions and data requests. Beta contacts are not subscribed to newsletters or used for advertising campaigns. At any time you can ask to stop receiving communications, leave the test and have your address, messages and attachments kept by the developer deleted (sections 7 and 8). Participation does not require buying the future app or guarantee that every suggestion will be implemented.

During testing, you may choose voluntarily to send the developer a file of your workouts (for example a route track) to help improve the app. This happens only if you actively send it yourself. In that case the developer will keep that file solely to analyze and improve the calculations, for the time needed for testing, and will delete it at your request. Please remove your name from files before sending them.

Support and encrypted diagnostic report. If you tap “Contact the Developer” in the app (Settings → Help and Support), from build 577 Charge first asks how you want to write. With Message only an email already addressed to the developer (carica.beta@gmail.com) opens without any attachment, and the app does not read Health data to prepare it; if the Mail app is not set up on your phone, Charge copies the address so you can write with the email app of your choice. Alternatively you can choose to attach a diagnostic report covering the last 28 days: first you read the explanation shown and switch on a separate explicit consent (Article 9(2)(a) GDPR), which starts switched off every time and is not remembered. Only then does Charge prepare the report on your phone and attach it, encrypted, to the email, which opens in Apple’s Mail app. You share it only if you tap Send: before that you can edit the message, remove the attachment or cancel, and nothing is sent automatically. If the Mail app is not set up on your phone, Charge offers to share only the encrypted attachment with the app of your choice and copies the developer’s address. Your choice, its time, the language and the exact consent text shown are included in the encrypted attachment: they are a record kept by the app, not a signature. You can withdraw this consent by writing to the same address with the report code: the report is then deleted.

Earlier beta versions. In versions from build 544 to 575, tapping “Contact the Developer” made Charge prepare the report immediately and attach it to the email, without the Message only choice and without a separate consent: sending was covered by the consent given at first launch (version 3), and you still had to tap Send, with the option to remove the attachment or cancel. In versions earlier than build 544 the support feature opened a plain email, without health data. The build number is shown in the version information in the app’s Settings. Anyone updating to build 577 accepts the terms and the consent again at first launch, without losing the Charge data already on the device.

Purpose: to understand and fix an unusual Charge value or a malfunction of the app. Contents of the attachment: your Charge for every 15-minute interval; heart rate, resting heart rate and heart rate variability; sleep and sleep stages; steps, calories and workouts (type, times, calories, average heart rate and distance); respiratory rate and blood oxygen, when available; your personal sleep and heart rate variability references and how far along their learning is (with the number of valid days, nights and measurements); age in years, sex, VO₂max and maximum heart rate; how much data is missing; model and version of your iPhone, Apple Watch and the app. It does not contain your name, your date of birth, your location, your routes, elevations or the countries you have visited. Encryption: the attachment is encrypted on your phone before it leaves the app and only the developer can open it, with a key kept outside the app; readable data is never written to your phone. The body of the email contains only the app version and build, the model and operating system of your devices, your time zone and a random code identifying the report, in addition to your email address and whatever you write yourself. The email travels through your email provider and the developer’s (currently Gmail).

The developer will use the diagnostic report and the message solely to reply to you and provide support, under the retention rules in section 7.

7. How long we keep data, and device backups

Applications and group administration. Applications that do not lead to participation are deleted from copies managed by the developer within 90 days of the last contact about the application. For participants, the address, invitation request and organizational correspondence are kept during participation and deleted within 90 days of its end. Requests to stop communications are respected without waiting for these deadlines. A request to delete your address, messages and attachments is handled without undue delay and in any case within the legal time limits (normally one month from the request), without waiting for the deadlines in this section; deletion is not instantaneous and concerns the copies managed by the developer. A minimal record of an objection may be kept only to prevent further contacts, for as long as necessary for that purpose. For people who make no request, the deletion periods in this section continue to apply.

Beta mailbox. The mailbox is not closed automatically at the end of development. If it is ever discontinued, another working contact for data requests will be given in this policy before it is discontinued; data already kept will continue to follow the deletion periods in this section.

Reports and TestFlight data. Ordinary support messages and identifiable suggestions are deleted from copies managed by the developer once the request is resolved and in any case within 90 days of receipt. A note of an idea may remain only after removing every element that could identify the person. The same 90-day limit from receipt or export applies to downloaded copies of technical TestFlight data. Tester removal and requests about data held in the platform are handled through the available Apple tools; data Apple retains for its own purposes follows Apple’s terms, not the periods for the developer’s copies. We cannot delete copies in the sender’s mailbox. Any retention required by law or necessary for a specific dispute is limited to indispensable data, with restricted access and only for the justified period.

Data processed on the device remains as long as the app is installed: it is deleted when you uninstall the app, or from the app’s Settings using full data deletion. Any files you voluntarily send us (section 6) are deleted at the end of the testing period or at your request. Support (section 6): the encrypted diagnostic report and any decrypted copies are kept only to handle the support request. They are deleted as soon as the analysis is complete and in any case no later than 90 days after receipt; they can be deleted earlier on request (section 8). The same limit applies to the support message received. Charge cannot delete copies left in your sent mail or on your email provider’s systems.

Device backups. Charge does not send your data to cloud services on its own initiative and does not use iCloud. Device backups managed by Apple (iCloud backup, backup to a Mac or PC) are a service you choose and control from your Apple settings. Since the version of October 19, 2026, Charge asks iOS to exclude from those backups all data that comes from Health, contains location or consists of physiological values (even if entered by hand, such as a manually entered VO₂max), including workout history and routes, downloaded terrain maps and place elevations, using the exclusion flag provided by the system. This is a request made to the system, not an absolute guarantee: Apple decides how to apply it. In the app’s ordinary settings, which are included in backups, Charge keeps only your name and preferences that contain no health or location data (for example light or dark appearance). After a restore or a change of phone, consent is normally requested again, and Charge rebuilds from Health data whatever is still available, as Apple restores it; a manually entered VO₂max and the coach history must be entered again or start over. Backups created before that version may contain the data the app kept at the time: the app does not modify or delete them (it has no access to backups), and you can manage them from your Apple settings. Full data deletion from the app acts on the device and does not modify existing backups.

8. Your rights

You have the right to access your data, correct it, delete it, restrict its use, object to its processing and withdraw your consent at any time. Since the data resides on your device, you can exercise most of these rights directly (by turning off permissions or uninstalling the app). From the app’s Settings you can also withdraw your consent at any time: Charge will then delete the data it has processed and stored on your device. This deletion does not affect Apple’s Health app, which always remains under your control according to your Apple settings. For any files, messages or diagnostic reports you have voluntarily sent to support, write to carica.beta@gmail.com (or to the working contact given in section 1, if the mailbox is replaced); for a diagnostic report, include the code shown in the email. At the same address you can ask at any time to stop receiving communications and to have your address, messages and attachments kept by the developer deleted, within the times described in section 7. Charge cannot delete copies left in your sent mail or on your email provider’s systems: you can delete them yourself.

You also have the right to lodge a complaint with the Italian Data Protection Authority (Garante per la protezione dei dati personali, www.garanteprivacy.it) or with the data protection authority of the country where you live or work.

9. Minors

The app is intended for adults. We do not knowingly collect data from anyone under 18.
